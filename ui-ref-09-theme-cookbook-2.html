00 — Why the first glass theme failed

Four rules for readable glass

Glassmorphism photographs well and reads badly. The failure is almost always the same: a saturated backdrop showing through a surface that is too transparent, with text that was picked for the mock rather than for contrast. Both glass themes below follow these rules.

× Saturated backdrop

Vivid purple and pink at full strength behind a 6% white surface leaves text sitting on an unpredictable, high-chroma field. Contrast varies across the same paragraph.

✓ Desaturate and darken the backdrop

Near-neutral slate with low-chroma tints at 24–30% opacity. The mesh still reads as atmosphere, but the value range behind any given card is narrow.

× Too transparent

Below roughly 6% white on dark, the surface stops occluding and the blur alone is carrying contrast — which it cannot do.

✓ Raise opacity, raise blur

7.5% white with a 26px blur on dark; 74% white with 22px on light. Add an inset 0 1px 0 highlight so the top edge reads as lit glass rather than fog.

× Body text on the mesh

Any text placed directly over the gradient will fail contrast somewhere, and you cannot test every position.

✓ Text only on cards

The mesh is background; every piece of content sits on a glass surface. Then contrast is a fixed, testable pair rather than a variable one.

Glass Refined — the corrected version

Glass Refined

The fix for the previous glass theme. Three changes: the backdrop is desaturated near-neutral slate instead of saturated purple and pink; surface opacity rises to 7.5% with a 26px blur so the panel actually occludes what is behind it; and body text sits at #b6bccb rather than a tinted mid-grey. Body copy never touches the mesh directly — it always sits on a card. That is the whole trick.

.th-glassr radius 16 · blur 26 · desaturated backdrop

Frost Light — white frosted

Frost Light

The light counterpart. White at 74% opacity over a soft blue-lilac wash, with a near-white inset highlight along the top edge. Light glass is easier to keep readable than dark glass because the surface is close to opaque; the blur is doing texture work rather than contrast work.

How to use this. Copy any token block over the [data-t="dark"] block in files 01–07 — components read only from tokens, so they re-skin without edits. Glass Refined is the only dark glass theme in the set; the earlier saturated version in file 08 was removed for readability. For an operations console, the practical shortlist is Fluent or Material for broad familiarity, Solarized for long-shift screens, Document for executive views, and High Contrast shipped alongside whichever you pick, since accessibility mode is usually a rollout requirement rather than an option.
