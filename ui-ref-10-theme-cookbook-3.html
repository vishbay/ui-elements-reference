Theme Cookbook III — SaaS, Utility, Paper, Carbon, HUD

Six themes chosen for what operations and SaaS teams actually ship. Studio Light and Midnight are the two to start from for a modern SaaS product: warm neutrals, a lit top edge on dark cards, black or cobalt primary, 8px radius, 150ms motion, nothing decorative. Then a monochrome utility register in the Linear / Vercel vein, a warm paper look for document tools, an IBM-Carbon-style layered system, and a restrained mission-control HUD. Same six axes as files 08 and 09; the demo also carries a toolbar strip.

⚠ Reduce Motion is on in your OS. CSS animations and transitions are being suppressed.
00 — Picking one

Nineteen themes, one decision

Across files 08, 09 and 10 there are now nineteen token sets. The choice is driven by who is looking, for how long, and what the product needs to feel like. Match on register first; every theme can be recoloured afterwards.

ProductStart fromWhy
Modern SaaS product, light.th-studio Warm off-white, black primary button, cobalt reserved for links, focus and the current item. Layered 1–3px shadows instead of borders doing all the work. This is the register of Linear, Attio and Stripe's dashboard.
Modern SaaS product, dark.th-midnight Blue-black ground, a one-pixel lit edge on every card, gradient-lit primary. Reads as a product rather than a terminal, without any glow, mesh or glass.
Internal ops console, mixed audience.th-console or .th-mono Console is the safe default. Mono removes the coloured accent entirely, which is the fastest way out of the generic look (see file 18).
NOC / trading / 24-hour screens.th-dense, .th-solar or .th-hud Low luminance, mono numerals, zero decoration. HUD adds a point of view; Dense adds density; Solarized adds fatigue resistance.
Enterprise deployment, IT-managed.th-carbon, .th-fluent or .th-material Familiar to the audience, defensible in procurement, and each has an open component library you can fall back to.
Knowledge / document tool.th-paper or .th-doc Serif display and warm neutrals read as writing rather than monitoring. Chrome disappears behind content.
Marketing-facing SaaS app.th-glassr or .th-frost Only where the product is the pitch. Use the corrected glass rules in file 09 and keep effects to one per screen.
Exported reports, print, executive views.th-print or .th-hc Black on white with hard rules survives photocopies and projectors. Ship High Contrast alongside any other pick.

Accent is optional

Mono Utility proves a theme can have no coloured accent at all. Semantic colours (good, warn, danger) still exist; they just stop competing with a brand colour.

Type does more than colour

Paper swaps one axis, the display face, and reads as a different product. Carbon swaps radius to 0 and depth to layers. Change one axis decisively before touching two.

Studio Light — modern SaaS, light

Studio Light

The look most current SaaS products converge on when they are designed rather than generated: warm off-white ground instead of pure white or grey, a black primary button with a one-pixel inner highlight, cobalt only for links and focus, and depth from two stacked one-to-three-pixel shadows rather than from borders. Tabular numerals, weight 600 instead of 700, letter-spacing slightly negative. Nothing here is an effect.

.th-studio radius 8 · ease-out 150ms · layered soft shadow · black primary
Midnight — modern SaaS, dark

Midnight

The dark counterpart. Blue-black rather than neutral black so text has somewhere to sit, each card carrying a one-pixel lit top edge (inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,.05)) that reads as material instead of as a flat rectangle, and a primary button lit from above with a subtle gradient. Inputs are darker than their card, which is the opposite of the light theme and the detail most dark modes miss. No glow, no glass, no mesh.

.th-midnight radius 8 · ease-out 160ms · lit top edge · cobalt primary
Mono Utility — no accent colour

Mono Utility

Greys only. The primary button is white on black, status colours are desaturated so they read as information rather than decoration, and everything moves in 120ms with no spring. Weight 560 instead of 700, letter-spacing slightly negative, nothing uppercase. This is the current default register for developer-facing SaaS and it is the hardest to mistake for a template.

.th-mono radius 6 · ease-out 120ms · hairline depth · white accent
Warm Paper — serif display

Warm Paper

Cream ground, a humanist sans for UI and an old-style serif for headings and numerals. Terracotta accent, low-saturation semantic colours. Radius 10, soft double shadow. Reads as a writing or knowledge tool rather than a monitoring one, which is exactly why it should not be used for an incident console.

.th-paper radius 10 · ease 240ms · soft shadow · serif display
Carbon Industrial — depth by layer

Carbon Industrial

Modelled on IBM's Carbon: radius 0 everywhere except tags, no card borders (each layer is a step lighter than the one below), inputs with a bottom rule only, buttons with the label left-aligned and room reserved for an icon, 110ms productive motion. Cobalt is the single accent. Reads as serious infrastructure tooling without any decoration at all.

.th-carbon radius 0 · productive 110ms · layered depth · Plex Sans
Mission-Control HUD — restraint version

Mission-Control HUD

The sci-fi console done with discipline: one cyan, one amber, a faint dot grid, corner ticks on cards instead of glow, and mono numerals everywhere a number appears. Uppercase tracked labels set the register; body copy stays sentence case. No scanlines, no animated borders, no bloom. If you add any of those, you have left restraint and entered a game menu.

.th-hud radius 2 · 200ms · corner ticks · mono numerals
How to use this. Copy any token block over the [data-t="dark"] block in files 01–07, exactly as with files 08 and 09. The toolbar strip (.d-bar, .d-tabs, .d-seg, .d-ico, .d-kbd) is new here and reads only from tokens, so it can be pasted into either earlier cookbook. Theme-specific overrides sit directly under each token block; they are the one or two rules that a token cannot express (Carbon's borderless layers, the HUD's corner ticks) and are deliberately kept to a handful.
