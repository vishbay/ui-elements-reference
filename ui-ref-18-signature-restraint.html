Signature & Restraint

Why a screen reads as AI-generated, and what to do instead. The fingerprint is a recognisable set of effects used together; the cure is an effect budget, a real type decision, one accent chosen on purpose, a grid you actually obey, and two or three detail signatures applied everywhere. This file makes each of those concrete.

⚠ Reduce Motion is on in your OS. CSS animations and transitions are being suppressed.
01 — The fingerprint

Same content, twice

Left is what a prompt produces when nobody decides anything: gradient headline, glowing borders, glass over an aurora, an emoji per card, a sparkle badge, everything centred and equal. Right is the same information with decisions made: left-aligned, one surface, hairline grid, tabular numbers, a single strong action. Neither uses a font that is not on your machine.

The tells, named

Gradient text, glow borders, glass on mesh, emoji as icons, sparkle badges, centred copy, three equal cards, pill CTAs with a bloom, and an adjective-heavy headline. Any two together are a fingerprint.

What replaced them

A page title with a monospace meta line, a status that means something, a bento of cells that are not the same size because the data is not the same importance, and a black primary button.

The underlying error

Decoration was doing the work that hierarchy should do. Once the hierarchy is right, the effects have nothing left to do and can be removed.

06 — Detail signatures

Pick two or three, apply them everywhere

A signature is a small decision repeated without exception. Each card shows the same detail done three ways; none is wrong. What is wrong is using all three in one product. Choose, then write it into the tokens.

Focus
Soft halo, hard offset ring, or an underline. The ring is the most distinctive and the most accessible.
Selection
Tint with bold text, full fill, or pill. The tint survives dense tables; the pill suits lists. No coloured edge bars.

07 — Before shipping

Twelve checks

Run through this on a screenshot, not the live app. Screenshots remove motion and hover, which is how most people will first see the product.

  1. Effect budget is two or fewer on every screen, and zero on data screens.
  2. The accent appears only on current nav, selected row, primary button and focus.
  3. No gradient text, no glow at rest, no emoji as icons.
  4. One display face at most, chosen deliberately, with the pairing written into tokens.
  5. Numbers are tabular in tables and KPIs; identifiers and timestamps are mono.
  6. Left-aligned everywhere except a login page and an empty state.
  7. Cards are not equal unless the data is. Size follows importance.
  8. Three surface levels only: page, surface, chrome. Overlays get the only large shadow.
  9. 8px grid holds for every gap, padding and control height; 4px only inside components.
  10. Two or three detail signatures chosen from section 06 and applied without exception.
  11. Copy is operational (file 17): what happened, what is safe, what to do next. No adjectives.
  12. Light and dark both pass contrast (file 13), and the theme was tested with real data, not lorem.
How this fits the set. File 01 is a catalogue of every effect; this file is the rule for spending them. Files 08–10 give you nineteen themes, of which Studio Light, Midnight, Mono Utility, Carbon, Dense Desk and HUD are already at or under budget. File 11 shows the rules applied to full screens. If a screen still looks generic after all twelve checks pass, the problem is the content: replace placeholder copy and even numbers with real, uneven data and look again.
